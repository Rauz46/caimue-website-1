# The exploded stack: rationale

**Idea.** Each card is a dark isometric floor, the same as the services section. A client's work stands on it as a thick exploded pile: base tile, then three separate slabs.

**Why it stops reading as dated.** The problem was never only the angle. Whole site screenshots carry their small text and their gradient heroes with them. So the crops were rethought: the top slab is the client's headline alone, large, like a printed sign. The middle slab is the picture. The bottom slab is a section of the work. No nav bars, no browser chrome, no paragraphs on the lead slab.

**Same hand as services.** Same view angle, same rounded tiles with a solid lavender edge, same purple and lavender base tiles, same faint floor grid on ink. Thickness is real geometry, stacked solid layers, so rounded corners stay correct when the stack turns.

**Light.** Contact shadows are drawn inside the face of the sheet below, so they stop at its edge and shift with the gap. Lower sheets get a violet multiply that lifts as the stack opens, so shade is never grey.

**Motion.** Everything comes from one value, distance to centre. Off centre the pile compresses and the words grow relative to the card, so they stay readable. At the centre the slabs rise and fan out. Pointer turns the focused stack up to 5 degrees. There is no idle motion.

**Any kind of work.** A sheet is only a source and a crop. A poster, deck page or film frame replaces a capture without any change to the structure.

**Discarded.** A white mat around each sheet (looked like tablet mockups). Full hero crops (still screenshots). A large base tile (blank field, ran into the words). A plumb line from the near corner (crossed the sentence).

**Weak point.** Isometric floating panels are a known SaaS illustration trope. The thick slabs and poster crops push against it but do not remove the risk. Slab type is also rotated, so it reads less directly than a straight-on image.
